**Introduction:** Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is increasingly conceptualized as a circuitopathy, in which early disruptions of large-scale functional brain networks interact with molecular pathology characterized by the accumulation of misfolded proteins. In particular, tau pathology shows a non-random spatial distribution that appears to be constrained by the large-scale functional network architecture, as assessed by resting-state functional connectivity (rs-fMRI). To date, the relationship between tau pathology and functional connectivity has been mainly investigated using static or dynamic functional connectivity approaches. While informative, these methods remain fundamentally pairwise, failing to capture potential coordinated interactions among multiple groups of brain regions (e.g., triplets). This limitation may contribute to the incomplete spatial correspondence between tau deposition and functional connectivity alterations observed across the AD continuum, particularly at the local level. In this context, higher-order connectomics, which model group-level interactions beyond pairwise dependencies, provide a means to capture instantaneous co-fluctuations across multiple brain regions. This represents a shift from local interactions to emergent system-level properties, enabling the characterization of network dynamics that cannot be reduced to edge-wise interactions. Here, we applied higher-order functional connectivity across the AD continuum to test the hypothesis that higher-order connectivity properties better capture disease progression and more closely align with the spatial distribution of tau pathology across different AD stages.

**Methods:** Data were obtained from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI). Participants spanned the full AD continuum, from cognitively unimpaired individuals with evidence of tau accumulation to subjects with subjective memory complaints, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and Alzheimer’s disease (AD). All participants had resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) and tau-PET acquisitions available within a two-year interval. Rs-fMRI data were preprocessed using standard pipelines, including motion correction, nuisance regression (motion and white matter/cerebrospinal fluid signals), temporal filtering, spatial normalization, and z-scoring of regional time series. The brain was parcellated into cortical and subcortical regions, yielding a multivariate time series of large-scale brain dynamics. Cortical areas were defined using the Yeo–Schaefer atlas (100 parcels), and subcortical regions using Tian’s atlas (16 regions). Tau-PET images were processed to extract regional measures of tau burden within each parcel. To move beyond pairwise connectivity, we applied a higher-order functional connectivity framework based on instantaneous co-fluctuation patterns across multiple brain regions. This approach models group-level interactions using simplicial complexes and captures emergent network dynamics that cannot be reduced to edge-wise relationships. We derived two metrics: hyper-coherence measures how strongly groups of brain regions fluctuate together; hyper-complexity which captures how structured and rich these group interactions are. These higher-order connectivity measures were related to regional tau-PET burden across the AD continuum, and a tripartite relationship among tau pathology, higher-order connectivity, and clinical/cognitive measures were assessed.

**Results:** We included n=213 individuals from the ADNI cohort. Hyper-coherence metrics did not show significant alterations in preclinical AD, while, as expected, they were significantly altered in clinically diagnosed AD compared to the other groups. These changes overlapped mainly with those detected using conventional metrics, suggesting a limited value of this metric over standard approaches. In contrast, hyper-complexity showed progressive pattern of alterations across the full AD continuum. These effects were predominantly localized within associative brain regions. Hyper-complexity showed a stronger spatial correspondence with tau-PET intensity in these regions. Finally, hyper-complexity mediated the relationship between tau burden and cognitive performance.

**Conclusions:** This study provides a proof-of-concept for extending connectomic analyses beyond pairwise correlations by introducing higher-order functional connectivity in the study of AD. Higher-order metrics capture aspects of large-scale network organization that are not detected by conventional connectivity measures and show a closer spatial relation with tau pathology across the AD continuum. By linking tau burden to alterations in network-level dynamical complexity and cognitive performance, higher-order connectivity may represent a sensitive systems-level marker of disease progression. These results have potential implications for pharmacological development and clinical trials, as higher-order connectivity metrics may offer novel biomarkers to assess target engagement and network-level drug effects. In particular, metrics indexing network complexity may be well suited to capture subtle modulatory effects of disease-modifying or off-label compounds acting on synaptic, neuromodulatory, or inflammatory mechanisms.
