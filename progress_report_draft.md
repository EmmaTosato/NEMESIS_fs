**Rationale & methods.** Stroke research usually treats anatomical (lesion), structural-disconnection (SDC), functional (fMRI) and physiological (EEG) signals as separate stories. The project (Corbetta Lab) tests instead whether they share the same latent structure, by reducing each modality independently to a low-dimensional embedding, clustering each one, and checking whether clusters overlap across modalities and relate to behavioral outcomes (NIHSS, language, neglect, motor). This builds on evidence (Corbetta 2015; Siegel et al. 2016; Griffis et al. 2019; Bisogno 2021; Talozzi et al. 2023) that lesion topography and disconnection are each intrinsically low-dimensional, and that disconnection explains cross-domain impairment better than raw lesion location. Five tasks: (1) embedding/clustering of lesions (n≈4000, 4 cohorts); (2) embedding of SDC (n≈3000); (3) fMRI-based clustering (n≈500) vs. healthy controls; (4) EEG embedding (n≈80, deferred to autumn 2026); (5) correlating multimodal features with behavioral outcomes.

A layered pipeline was built: a BIDS-compliant retrieval layer harmonizes four cohorts (WashU, PASPORT, PSP, UKLFR — 1150 subjects with validated lesion masks so far); a feature layer builds voxel-wise or atlas-parcellated lesion matrices (a combined 372-region Glasser+Harvard-Oxford atlas reproduces Thiebaut de Schotten et al. 2020's parcellation); an analysis layer performs dimensionality reduction (PCA, PCA-varimax, UMAP, t-SNE, PaCMAP) then clustering (KMeans, Agglomerative, GMM, HDBSCAN, Spectral), keeping fine-tuning strictly separate from frozen, human-decided production runs. SDC computation relies on BCBToolKit/BCBlib, SLURM-parallelized with per-subject validation; FC features are masked per-subject by lesion extent, following Siegel et al. 2016.

**Progress & results.** Retrieval is stable and in production; the combined atlas is built and validated. The dim.-reduction/clustering pipeline (Task 1) is most mature: UMAP, t-SNE, PaCMAP production runs identify a robust k≈4 solution under UMAP (silhouette-confirmed on KMeans/GMM), while plain PCA is confounded by lesion volume (r=0.92 with PC2) and excluded. Default Euclidean distance on binary lesion masks proved itself volume-dominated; Jaccard/Dice normalization fixed this but showed the dominant Jaccard split reflects lesion laterality — a known covariate, not a novel pattern. DBSCAN was replaced by HDBSCAN after proving unreliable across most combinations. FC lesion-masking (Task 3) was run on the full WashU cohort (169 subjects with both lesion and FC). BCBToolKit (Task 2) was validated end-to-end on a real subject via SLURM; full-cohort orchestration is in progress.

**Difficulties.** Pitfalls caught before/after production: a scoring function silently using the wrong distance metric; a plotting bug slicing multi-dimensional embeddings as if ordered by importance (true only for PCA); silent third-party bugs (integer overflow, dropped regions). BCBToolKit's cluster-only dependency blocks local testing and needed a non-standard local install (missing server permissions, request pending; a glibc login/compute-node mismatch caused an early failure). Dataset heterogeneity (mixed patient/control populations under shared trees) required repeated discovery-layer fixes.

**Future steps.** Once Task 2 embeddings are available, cross-check lesion-based vs. SDC-based clusters (contingency table, agreement metrics) and test the expected many-to-one convergence — distinct lesion topographies sharing a disrupted pathway should collapse onto fewer SDC clusters. Characterize clusters by mean lesion/disconnectome maps and relate them to NIHSS/behavioral domains, testing whether sensorimotor deficits track lesion topography while associative/memory deficits track (dis)connectivity (Siegel et al. 2016). Also: consensus/stability clustering (RSC; Monti et al. 2003) for cluster-count validation; scale Task 3 once FC matrices cover all 12 atlas combinations.
