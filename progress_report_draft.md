**Rationale & methods.** Stroke research typically treats anatomical (lesion), structural-disconnection (SDC), functional (fMRI) and physiological (EEG) signals as separate stories. The project (Corbetta Lab) tests instead whether they share the same latent structure — i.e. describe the same underlying deficit — by reducing each modality independently to a low-dimensional embedding, clustering each one, and testing whether clusters overlap across modalities and jointly relate to behavioral outcomes (NIHSS, language, neglect, motor domains). This builds on evidence (Corbetta 2015; Siegel et al. 2016; Griffis et al. 2019; Bisogno 2021; Talozzi et al. 2023) that lesion topography and disconnection are each intrinsically low-dimensional, and that disconnection better explains cross-domain impairment than raw lesion location. Work spans five tasks: (1) embedding/clustering of lesions (n≈4000, 4 cohorts); (2) embedding of structural disconnectomes (SDC, n≈3000); (3) fMRI-based clustering (n≈500) against healthy controls; (4) EEG embedding (n≈80, deferred to autumn 2026); (5) correlating multimodal features with behavioral outcomes.

A layered pipeline was built: a BIDS-compliant retrieval layer harmonizes four heterogeneous cohorts (WashU, PASPORT, PSP, UKLFR — 1150 subjects with validated lesion masks so far); a feature layer builds voxel-wise or atlas-parcellated lesion matrices (a combined 372-region Glasser+Harvard-Oxford atlas reproduces Thiebaut de Schotten et al. 2020's parcellation); an analysis layer performs dimensionality reduction (PCA, PCA-varimax, UMAP, t-SNE, PaCMAP) then clustering (KMeans, Agglomerative, GMM, HDBSCAN, Spectral), keeping exploratory fine-tuning strictly separate from frozen production runs, always human-decided. Structural disconnectome computation relies on BCBToolKit/BCBlib (staging→disconnectome→parcellated-features, SLURM-parallelized, per-subject validation); functional-connectivity features are masked per-subject by lesion extent, following Siegel et al. 2016.

**Progress & results.** The retrieval pipeline is stable and in production; the combined atlas is built and validated. The dimensionality-reduction/clustering pipeline (Task 1) is the most mature component: production runs across UMAP, t-SNE and PaCMAP identify a robust k≈4 solution under UMAP (silhouette-confirmed on KMeans/GMM), while plain PCA is confounded by lesion volume (r=0.92 with PC2) and was excluded. The default Euclidean distance on binary lesion masks was itself found volume-dominated; Jaccard/Dice normalization removed this confound but revealed that the dominant split under Jaccard reflects lesion laterality — a known covariate, not a novel pattern. DBSCAN was replaced by HDBSCAN after proving unreliable across most reduction/metric combinations. Functional-connectivity lesion-masking (Task 3) was run on the full WashU cohort (169 subjects with both lesion and FC data). BCBToolKit (Task 2) was installed and validated end-to-end on a real subject via SLURM; full orchestration across the cohort is in progress.

**Difficulties.** Non-obvious pitfalls caught before/after production: a scoring function silently comparing embeddings to the wrong distance metric; a plotting bug slicing multi-dimensional embeddings as if ordered by importance (true only for PCA); silent third-party bugs (integer overflow, dropped regions in masking). BCBToolKit's cluster-only dependency blocks local testing and required a non-standard local install (missing server permissions, shared-setup request pending; a glibc mismatch between login/compute nodes caused an early failure). Dataset heterogeneity (mixed patient/control populations under shared directory trees) required repeated fixes to the discovery layer.

**Future steps.** Once Task 2 embeddings are available, cross-check lesion-based and SDC-based clusters (contingency table, agreement metrics) and test the expected many-to-one convergence — distinct lesion topographies sharing a disrupted pathway should collapse onto fewer SDC clusters. Characterize each cluster by its mean lesion/disconnectome map and relate it to NIHSS and behavioral domains, testing whether sensorimotor deficits track lesion topography while associative/memory deficits track (dis)connectivity (Siegel et al. 2016). Also: consensus/stability clustering (RSC; Monti et al. 2003) to validate cluster counts beyond single-run indices; scale Task 3 once FC matrices cover all 12 atlas combinations.
